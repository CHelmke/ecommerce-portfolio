# Case Study: Why Similar Amazon Products May Sell Differently

*Public portfolio note: product identifiers are sanitized; the method and snapshot metrics are preserved.*

A customer-facing PDP can reveal useful hypotheses, but it cannot prove causation. My approach is to identify visible differences, normalize misleading comparisons, and then use Seller Central data to confirm what is actually driving the performance gap.

## 1. Normalize the offer
The products serve different guest counts, so total price alone is not a fair comparison. Price per guest and price per piece provide a more defensible value view.

## 2. Review search presentation
The main image is part of both discoverability and conversion. A soft or visually muddy image may reduce click-through in search and make the bundle harder to understand on the PDP.

## 3. Read social proof carefully
Star rating, review count, Best Sellers Rank and Amazon badges are different signals. I use them as supporting evidence, not interchangeable measurements of unit sales.

## 4. Audit catalog structure
Structured fields such as Number of Items, Material, Included Components and variation relationships can affect shopper clarity, browse/filter behavior and catalog quality.

## 5. Check offer and delivery under the same conditions
Party supplies are event-date purchases. Delivery promise, inventory and Featured Offer eligibility can matter as much as content, so both ASINs should be checked with the same location and timing.

## 6. Confirm with internal data
If the weaker ASIN receives less traffic, investigate discoverability and advertising. If traffic is comparable but conversion is weaker, prioritize price, value communication, images, offer, reviews and variation structure.
